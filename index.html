FIELD NOTES AV-7 VOLTMETER · PS-30 SUPPLY · BENCH SIMULATION

1 · POWER UP & MAINS PRACTICE

Flip OUTPUT on the PS-30 to energise the circuit. Choose a range at or above the expected voltage — 12 V belongs on the 25 V range. In AC mode the supply sweeps all the way to 300 V RMS: mains territory. Rehearse 120 V and 230 V readings on the 250 V or 500 V range. The movement is ballistic: it overshoots, settles, and pegs hard past full scale when you guess low. This is a safe simulation — real mains is lethal and demands CAT-rated probes and proper training.

2 · READING THE SCALE

Three arcs live on the face — 0–2.5, 0–10 and 0–50. The active arc is printed in full white; the others fade. Multiply what you read by the range factor (shown in the status bar).

230 V on the 250 V range → read the outer 0–2.5 arc, ×100. Needle on 2.3 reads 230 V.

3 · PARALLAX & THE MIRROR

Sweep your pointer across the meter face: the reflection in the mirror strip drifts away from the needle. Only when ghost and needle touch is your eye square to the scale — that is exactly what the mirror was invented for.

4 · PROBES & POLARITY

Drag a probe tip onto the other binding post to swap the leads — double-clicking a plug works too. Reversed DC drives the needle left of zero; AC does not care about polarity.

5 · ZERO & SPAN CALIBRATION

Zero: with the supply off, drag the slotted ZERO ADJ screw on the face until the needle rests exactly on 0.
Span: press CAL REF (precision 2.500 V), select the 2.5 V range in DC function, and trim SPAN CAL until the needle sits on 2.5.
Press DRIFT to age the movement, then recalibrate. The CROSS-CHECK readout in the status bar tells you how close you got.

6 · AC/DC CROSS-MODE

  • DC function on an AC source → the movement averages: the needle quivers near zero.
  • AC function on a DC source → the rectifier is average-responding but sine-calibrated: reads ≈ 11 % high.
  • AC ranges are polarity-blind — swapping the probes changes nothing on ~.
